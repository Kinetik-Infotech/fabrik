# Routines and their design

A routine is a piece of work you do again and again, such as invoicing or planning the week's meals. It can be for your business or your own life. fabrik writes down how you do it today, helps you design a better way, and Claude follows the design you choose.

These need a Code session in your `fabrik` folder, where fabrik's assistant has the routine skills.

## Getting started

Say "Let's get started". Claude first asks whether fabrik is for a business, your own life, or both. For a business, it reads your website and social media profiles, then asks about your objectives. For the objective you pick, it writes down how its routine runs today, and which applications must stay. Then it designs a better way, and once you approve it, Claude does that work by the new routine.

At the end, Claude lists the objectives and routines still to do, and you can pick one up in any later session. You can stop after any step, and Claude carries on from what it saved.

## Your business

Claude first reads your business profile: what you sell, your industry, your size, your customers and the applications you use. With none, it asks for your website and social media profiles, and fills the profile and your brand from what they say. You correct anything it read wrong. Then it asks only for what the pages didn't say, one question at a time, and saves each answer.

## Your objectives

An objective is the outcome you want from one routine, such as "triage my email", "plan the family's meals each week", "automate creating invoices" or "create content for my social channels". Claude saves each one as a record of its own, marked personal or business, with how you'll know it's achieved. Each objective names its routine, and the routine's purpose statement names the objective.

## Writing down a routine

Say "Let's write down how I do my invoicing". Claude asks one question per reply, and saves each answer as evidence. For each application you use, it asks whether it must stay or could be replaced. Once you confirm the steps, it saves the routine as a model of how it runs today, and draws it.

## Why it exists

Ask "Why do I do this?" Claude asks why five times over, and writes a purpose statement: who it is for, the real problem and how you will measure success.

## A better way

Say "Design a better way". Claude questions each step against your business and what is usual in your industry. It takes stock of the connectors and plugins you have or could add. It keeps each application you said must stay. It saves the design as a model, with each step assigned to you, to code, to a connector or to Claude.

## The routine in force

Once you choose a design, it becomes the version in force. Whenever Claude does that work for you, it finds the version in force, names it, and follows its steps. A step assigned to you stays yours: Claude prepares it and leaves it for you.

A design never makes anything run on its own. Each step that sends, pays, books or deletes waits for your approval in the session.

## A printed guide to a routine

Ask for the routine's standard operating procedure as a PDF, and Claude prints its steps from the version in force.
